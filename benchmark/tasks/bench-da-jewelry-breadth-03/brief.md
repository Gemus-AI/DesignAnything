AFTERIMAGE | Articulated shoulder and waist jewelry for live performance

We are commissioning a coordinated body-jewelry set for AFTERIMAGE, an independent stage work in which a performer's gestures leave a visual echo. The set consists of one shoulder assembly S with left and right parts and one waist assembly W. The adult performer is 1700 mm tall, with shoulder breadth 400 mm and natural-waist circumference 760 mm. The existing plain black sleeveless unitard is the costume foundation. Our fixed starting point is jewelry whose articulated elements continue the line of a gesture: at least one moving decorative element on each shoulder and one at the waist. Develop this direction through a materials and craft note before drawing the applications. Print the production name as "AFTERIMAGE" and the descriptor as "Echo in motion" on the note, the performance hero and the presentation cover.

S attaches at SL and SR, the performer's left and right shoulder tops; W attaches at WL and WR, the lateral natural waist, and WB, the center back waist. These five reinforced textile regions take the loops, ties or releasable closures you design. S has a maximum outward extension of 100 mm beyond either shoulder; W has a maximum extension of 80 mm from the waist body surface and 200 mm below the natural waist, in every pose. Keep the face, neck and armpit movement visibly clear. The material palette is anodized aluminum, optional translucent PET sheet, woven textile tape for flexible connections and silicone for body-facing closure pads. Establish thicknesses, colors, finishes and joint details in the opening note. The movement sequence is P0, front-facing with arms down; P1, front-facing with arms extended sideways at shoulder height; and P2, turned approximately 45 degrees to the performer's right with both arms extended forward at shoulder height. Lighting cue L1 is warm amber from the front with soft neutral fill; L2 is cool blue rear/side rim light with dim neutral front fill.

Audience: the adult performer, choreographer and costume workshop, for selecting the set's appearance, reviewing its movement logic and planning prototype rehearsals.
