OFFCUT THREE | Stackable visitor stools from allocated timber

We need three identical visitor stools for our shared design studio, identified as A, B and C. The complete batch comes from three dry, planed hardwood panels P1, P2 and P3, each 400 by 400 by 20 mm, and twelve hardwood billets B01 through B12, each 30 by 30 by 560 mm. The species and structural grade are unknown. Every blank must contribute to the stools. On each panel, the closed corner region X 360..400 and Y 330..400 is waste through the full thickness; retained wood must lie where X < 360 or Y < 330. Billet material beyond L 500 is waste, comprising the 15 mm buffer and defect at L 515..560. Every separating saw cut consumes 3 mm. Our additional allowance is 36 M6 bolts of nominal 60 mm length, 36 matching nuts, 72 washers, twelve mild-steel connector blanks of 80 by 80 by 3 mm, twelve felt squares of 80 by 80 by 3 mm, wood adhesive and water-based finish. Develop the form and part arrangement within this stock, using a clear water-based finish that leaves the grain visible.

Each stool needs a seat top 440–470 mm above the floor, a clear horizontal seating square of 300 by 300 mm and a footprint within 550 by 550 mm. Store the three complete stools in one upright nested stack within 600 mm wide by 600 mm deep by 1050 mm high. Designed bearing contacts support each upper stool; every other close approach between stools needs at least 5 mm clearance. A sits above B above C and is lifted off first, then returned as a complete stool. Our existing worktable is 1800 by 800 mm, with its top at 740 mm and clear underside at 710 mm. Show one adult on each stool at three distinct table positions and a continuous route at least 750 mm wide to storage. Print "OFFCUT THREE" on the stool views and stock account, and "Lift A first" on the retrieval sequence.

Audience: the studio owners and furniture maker, for choosing the stool design and organising the three-stool build and storage arrangement.
