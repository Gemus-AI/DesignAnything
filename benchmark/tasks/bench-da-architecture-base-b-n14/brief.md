COMMON REPAIR | An industrial hall for neighbourhood repair and making

We are converting our former assembly hall into a community workshop for household repairs, shared tools and small-scale making. The direction is fixed: exposed existing brickwork and steel roof trusses, repaired concrete flooring, and new freestanding rooms with birch plywood linings and glazed upper walls. The site measures 30000 × 24000 mm; the single-storey hall measures 24000 × 12000 mm and stands 3000 mm from the west, east and north boundaries. Its south yard is 9000 mm deep. Ground floor and yard share ±0.000; truss underside, eaves and ridge are +4.800, +5.400 and +6.900 respectively. Structural spacing is four bays of 5850 mm with an 11400 mm transverse span. The footprint, ten columns, five trusses, brick envelope, three door openings, two south windows and five rooflights stay in their recorded positions.

Organise Reception, Teaching, Making, Tool Store, Intake, Collection, Accessible Toilet and Staff Room around a public route at least 1500 mm clear. Visitors enter through D-01; repair items arrive through D-02 and move through Intake, Making and Collection. Tool lending remains visible from Reception. Course Day accommodates 12 learners, 8 makers and 2 operators; Open Workshop accommodates 6 learners, 8 makers and 2 operators. The building closes completely from 5 October to 13 December 2026 and reopens as one completed workshop on 14 December 2026. Print "COMMON REPAIR" and "Repair together" at the public entrance. Use "Course Day" and "Open Workshop" as the headings of their respective occupied views.

Audience: neighbourhood residents and workshop operators, for the layout decision and the refurbishment brief.
