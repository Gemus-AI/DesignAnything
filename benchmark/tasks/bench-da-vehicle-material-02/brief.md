CROSSWATER | Short-trip commuter ferry cabin

Develop the complete enclosed passenger saloon of Ferry EUREKA, HAER CA-59, as a contemporary short-trip commuter cabin. The horizontal shell is fixed by the attached passenger-deck plan: the two transverse sliding-door partitions, four saloon openings, window-wall rhythm, columns, kingposts and central enclosures all stay in their recorded positions. The design covers both sides of the central bodies. Page-left is the Oakland end; page-right is the San Francisco end. These names provide cabin orientation. The exterior end decks retain their recorded stairs, railings and boundaries. Use the plan's original scale bar for horizontal dimensions, with 1 foot equal to 304.8 mm; identify traced values as approximate archive-derived dimensions. The 1988 photographs supply visible architectural relationships, and existing vertical dimensions remain unmeasured.

We need 120 seats in 60 two-person modules, 24 separately marked standing places and 12 bag positions numbered B01-B12. Each sitting surface is nominally 480 mm wide and 440 mm deep, at 450 mm above its local floor. Standing markers are nominally 550 by 550 mm, each served by a proposed handhold; the handhold system includes grasp surfaces at 950 mm and 1100 mm above the local floor. Each bag position accommodates an upright bag measuring 300 mm wide, 200 mm deep and 450 mm high. A continuous proposed 1200 mm clear circulation band connects all four opening approaches around both sides of the central bodies; the existing door throats retain their archived widths. Give commuters carrying bags a clear relationship between seats, standing places, storage and boarding order. Use recycled PET upholstery, aluminium supports and handholds, rubber bag-contact liners and floor surfaces, and printed aluminium information panels in a contemporary material system. Print "CROSSWATER" on the information surface of the fixed newsstand enclosure. Passenger wording is fixed as "Alight first", "Then board", "Seating", "Standing", "Bags", "Oakland end" and "San Francisco end".

Audience: the cabin design team and project owner, choosing the complete interior arrangement, material system and passenger information for further development.
