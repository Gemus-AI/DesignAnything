SALT MOON | A science fiction world shaped by tides and working lives

We are developing the world art for SALT MOON, a survival crafting game for players who explore by learning how a place works. Its surface seas have evaporated: Salt Flats cover an underground Undersea, and fallen ring sections form the Orbital Ruins. The player is a scout of the Tidal Council. Expeditions follow an approximately 40-minute tide cycle: low tide opens the sinkholes, high tide floods the lower Ruins and raises airlocks within reach, and turning tides bring salt storms. The working loop is scout, gather, return to the Council hold, craft and unlock the next region's gear.

Build on the attached region paintings. Salt Flats uses salt glass and #D8CDA8; Undersea uses pressure shells and #397C86; Orbital Ruins uses scarred alloy and #766B83. Repaired equipment shares matte brine-metal fittings, woven fibre straps and the Council's split-circle badge. Use broad opaque brushwork, chipped material edges and a dominant light from the upper left. The playable footprints are respectively 48000 × 32000 mm, 36000 × 24000 mm and 24000 × 32000 mm. Our scout, C01, is 1720 mm tall; the Tide Gauge, P01, measures 240 × 90 × 45 mm.

Every character and prop sheet contains front, side and rear views, its two recorded states, a close-up and a small map icon. Carry those identities into the scenes and film. Print "SALT MOON" on scene plates and interaction sheets; use the prompts "Read tide" and "Collect sample" on interaction sheets. The shared interaction snapshot is Low Tide, 08:00 remaining, with Samples 3 / 12.

Audience: exploration-focused players and our world, character and environment leads, supporting the visual direction decision and the first playable region build.
