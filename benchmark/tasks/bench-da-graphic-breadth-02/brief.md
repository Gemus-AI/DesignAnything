ORBIT YEAR | A year of astronomy in public view

Meridian City Planetarium is commissioning the posters and digital graphics for ORBIT YEAR, our 2027 public learning program. The four themes are The changing Moon, January–March 2027; When shadows align, April–June 2027; One tilt, two seasons, July–September 2027; and A ruler for space, October–December 2027. These are quarterly learning periods. Build the series around the supplied institution mark and its color #18324A, with a shared explanation hierarchy and a distinct visual treatment for each theme. Every theme poster and digital adaptation carries "ORBIT YEAR", Meridian City Planetarium, its theme title and its program period. The invitation "Find the pattern. Ask the next question." is available as optional copy. Posters are A1, 594 × 841 mm, with 3 mm bleed and a minimum 5 mm internal safe margin. Digital applications use a landscape screen and square social posts, each with a 5% safe inset on every edge.

Scientific relationships are the backbone of the series. Moonlight is reflected sunlight; the changing view of the illuminated half produces a phase cycle of about 29.5 days. Eclipse graphics distinguish the Moon's shadow reaching Earth from Earth's shadow reaching the Moon, and explain why the tilted lunar orbit makes eclipses occasional. Seasonal graphics pair northern and southern conditions around June and December while preserving Earth's axial direction. The distance comparison uses approximately 150 million kilometres per AU, with the Sun at 0, Earth at 1 and Saturn at 9.5 on a linear ruler. Exaggerated explanatory geometry carries "Schematic — not to scale"; the ruler carries "Distance scale in AU; body symbols not to scale". These labels also accompany diagrams embedded in posters and digital artwork.

Audience: members of the public, teenagers and accompanying adults will use the series to understand basic astronomy, while the planetarium's education and communications teams will use the work to approve the year's public campaign.
